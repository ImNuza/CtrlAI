Muted Botanical Design System

A quiet, editorial visual language drawn from 18th–19th century botanical illustration and pixel-measured muted herbarium photography. All icons are custom SVG — no emoji. Every decision is senior-safe: large text, large tap targets, warm neutrals, no shame.

Colour system

All colours measured from real botanical source imagery (k-means pixel clustering). Maximum saturation ~45%. Shadows always warm taupe or deep olive — never cool grey or pure black.

Typography system

Two-font system: Fraunces (display/headings — soft irregular serif, organic editorial feel) + Work Sans (UI body — humanist warm sans). Pinyon Script is used only for botanical plant name labels. Avoid geometric sans (Poppins, Montserrat) and all hand-drawn/cartoonish faces.

SVG symbols & icons

All icons rendered as inline SVG, warm-toned, never filled with pure black. Stroke weight 1.5–2px, colour follows the foliage palette. Icon minimum touch target 64×64px even if the glyph is smaller. All icons are self-contained SVG — no emoji anywhere in the game UI.

Plant art direction

Classical botanical illustration style: fine sepia/black ink outlines, soft watercolour washes, cross-hatching for volume. High-key overall (mean value 83%), max saturation ~45%. Never pure black shadows — resolve to warm taupe or deep olive.

Kopitiam Corner

The fifth island, ported from the round-one Mahjong Kakis prototype. Face-up tiles, tap two that match, clear the table. Three companions share one speech bubble and take turns. There is no timer anywhere in this game. The only counter is taps since the last match, and all it does is ask a kaki to say something.

The three kakis

Only one talks at a time. Affinity decides who owns a moment — Lily softens a near miss, Beng crows over a win, Rose nudges — everything else rotates, and nobody speaks twice in a row. The rotation is seated off the visit count, so a different face says hello each time the player comes back.

Lily
warm, flowery
Beng
cheeky, kopi

Lily wears the speaking state above: a 4px accent ring and a 4px lift. The name goes bold and turns kopi brown. Two portraits can never wear it at once.

The shared bubble

No line ever erases itself. A kaki's words stay on the table until the next line replaces them or the player taps the bubble to put it away, and putting a line away is a fade, not a delete. The words stay in the box so the box keeps its height and the tiles below do not move a pixel.

Auntie Lily
You came back Ah Ma. Last time we played a few rounds together.

The pointer is two stacked triangles, outer in the border colour and inner in the bubble face, positioned under whichever portrait is speaking. The slot underneath it only ever grows: game.js measures it after every line and writes the tallest it has seen this deal into --kaki-slot-floor, so a shorter line afterwards cannot pull the table up under the player's finger. The floor clears on a new deal, which is the one moment the player expects the table to change.

Tiles

Twelve identities: dots 1–4, bamboo 1–4, and the four winds. Plain SVG in a 60×66 box so the faces scale with the tile. Every tile is 64px minimum and face up from the deal — nothing is hidden, so this is recognition, not recall.

Three dots, two bamboo, east wind, and on the right a tile in the selected state — kopi border, tinted face, 4px lift, deeper lip. The table itself is light marble: two soft veins and a corner sheen over bg-surface.

Player bar
Ah Ma 2 of 4 pairs

The name wins the space fight — it is the whole promise of the game, and tapping it reopens the name chooser without throwing away the round in progress. The dot row is aria-hidden decoration repeating the sentence beside it word for word, which is why the dots stand down below 24rem and never the name.

Behaviour rules
MomentWhat happensWhat never happens
MismatchBoth tiles wiggle, a kaki says something warm, tiles return face up after 600msNo error state, no red, no score penalty shown
Six taps, no matchA kaki offers a thought. Twice running and one real pair breathes for 900msNothing on screen calls it a hint or help
DifficultyThe level sets a base pair count; recent miss rate moves the real wall one pair either side and thins or crowds the lookalike tilesNo level number, badge or label ever names it
WaitingCounted in taps since the last matchNever a clock. There is no timer in this game
Coming backA kaki greets the player by name with a warm phrase from their historyNever a statistic, never a count of what they got wrong
