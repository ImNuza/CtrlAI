The game as agreed

Reconciled from Zoe's PDF plus the decisions made after she generated it. This is the current shared understanding, stated plainly so we can argue with it.

Age Well: Garden of Life. A gentle wellness game for older adults built on a farming-sim loop, positioned in the PDF as inspired by Roblox's Grow a Garden.

The 40-point gap

The finding

Use of AI Tools is worth 40 of 100 points. The rubric names worldbuilding and intelligent NPCs as its Game Track examples. Garden of Life, as specified, contains no AI anywhere in the product.1

Read the six mechanics again. Quiz, gacha, plant, walk, water, meal card. Every one of them is a deterministic if-statement. The only AI in the plan is that we will use CodeBuddy to write the code and Miora to draw the vegetables, which is the same sentence every other team will write.

The sting is that we already had three AI stories and traded all of them away. Memory Garden composed a plant procedurally from what a senior told it. Mahjong Kakis had three characters who remembered your last visit. Scam Dojo generated fresh scam scripts every run. Zoe herself opened this meeting, at 01:16, pitching an AI NPC that asks about your day. That thread never came back.

This is not an argument for scrapping the garden. Team agreement with nine days left is worth more than a marginally better concept, and the garden is genuinely the strongest theme fit we have found. It is an argument that the garden is currently half a submission, and the missing half is the half worth 40 points.

What is strong

Gardening is the best-evidenced choice on the whole list

This is worth knowing because it is a real asset we have not used. Horticultural therapy for older adults has multiple systematic reviews and meta-analyses behind it, showing benefits for depression, emotional wellbeing, social function and quality of life, and reduced agitation in people with dementia.2 Compare that with brain training, covered below. We stumbled into the well-supported one.

The Singapore hook is sitting right there, unused

NParks' Community in Bloom has over 2,000 registered gardening groups and more than 48,000 gardeners, plus over 2,500 allotment plots that residents can lease.3 Seniors are central to it. There are also NParks Therapeutic Gardens designed for older users. A digital garden in Singapore is not a random theme, it maps onto something a real ah ma in Tampines actually does on a Saturday morning. Nobody said this in the meeting and it should be on slide two.

Meal achievements are the most original thing in the spec

Rewarding crop variety rather than crop profit is quietly clever. It makes the optimal play also the healthy play with no lecturing. Keep it and make it the centrepiece.

The team argued its way to the right cruelty setting

Zoe wanted plants to die for engagement, Matthew said that defeats the point, they settled on wilting. That is exactly the call our own UX floor demands: no fail states that shame the player. Good instinct, arrived at without anyone quoting the rule.

Focus, and no waiting

One game beats three. Starting before the credits arrive beats blocking on someone else's Telegram reply. Both correct.

What will cost us

1. Quiz-to-earn is the wrong front door fix

Zoe's unresolved objection has research behind it. Surveys of older adults across the US, Canada, South Korea and Flanders find a strong preference for puzzle, card, tile and board games. Match-three titles like Bejeweled and Candy Crush are the genre seniors actually open.4 Quizzes are the format they gravitate to least, and we put one in front of the entire economy. Zoe's exact instinct in the meeting, that elderly players reach for chess, Candy Crush and Zuma rather than Duolingo, is what the literature says.

2. The cognitive-decline claim is a liability cut

The PDF's impact table claims quizzes produce "lower population-level risk of cognitive decline". The evidence does not support that. Meta-analyses of commercial brain-training find modest gains that mostly vanish once you adjust for publication bias, with only processing speed surviving, and no significant improvement at all in older adults who already have cognitive impairment.5 Lumosity paid the FTC two million dollars in 2016 for advertising almost exactly this claim, specifically for suggesting its games could stave off memory loss, dementia and Alzheimer's without the science to back it.6

Any judge with a health background will spot it. Take the sentence out of the deck. We do not need it, because the gardening evidence is real.

3. Gacha is the one thing that can sink us cut

Zoe read the warning out loud in the meeting, at 51:15, from her own Claude session: remove randomized loot entirely, predatory monetization is especially risky for older or cognitively vulnerable players. Matthew joked past it and gacha stayed in the PDF as step two of the core loop.

The concern is real. Randomized-reward mechanics are designed around anticipation and reinforcement loops that disproportionately affect vulnerable players, and several jurisdictions now regulate them as gambling.7 There is an extra wrinkle worth naming: China's own regulator does not approve games containing a compulsion loop, and we are pitching to Tencent. Putting a slot machine in a social-good game for cognitively vulnerable seniors is the single easiest thing for a judge to point at.

4. Step quests cannot work from a browser link cut

This one is a hard technical fact, not an opinion. Our deliverable is a game web link. Browsers cannot reach the hardware step counter. Native apps use CMPedometer on iOS and TYPE_STEP_COUNTER on Android, both running on a low-power coprocessor that keeps counting with the phone asleep. A web page has none of that. The best it can do is read the accelerometer through DeviceMotionEvent, which since iOS 13 requires a permission request triggered by a user gesture and stops the moment the tab is backgrounded.8

"Walk 1,000 steps to unlock a seed" is a slide, not a feature. If it appears in the demo video and a judge opens the link, it will not work.

5. Water reminders will not fire for a judge fix

Web push on iOS only works if the user has added the site to their Home Screen first. An open Safari tab does not count.9 A judge clicking our link gets no notifications at all. Worse, "add to home screen" is a real barrier for the actual target user. Keep the reminder as an in-game beat, show it in the video, and stop describing it as the differentiator.

6. Healthy 365 already does most of this, in Singapore, for this exact age group fix

This is the most dangerous overlap and nobody raised it. HPB's Healthy 365 app has run the National Steps Challenge since 2015: clock steps, earn Healthpoints, redeem vouchers. It now includes a dedicated programme for people aged 50 and over built around one simple task a day, customised workouts and rewarding quizzes.10 A population-level evaluation covered nearly 700,000 adults.

Steps for points, plus quizzes for rewards, aimed at older Singaporeans, is the government app that already exists on their phone. Local judges will know it. We cannot win on that ground and should not try to fight there.

7. We hand judges the "it is a clone" line ourselves fix

The PDF's subtitle says inspired by Roblox's Grow a Garden. Project Quality scores creativity for 30 points. Do not put the comparison in our own document. Rename the game, and make the differences the first thing on the page.

8. React Native does not produce a web link fix

The call decided React for both iPhone and Android. But the submission deliverable is a game web link, an online demo playable in a browser, with EdgeOne as the suggested host. If that means React Native, we ship something the judges cannot open. Build a mobile web app, phone-sized, installable as a PWA if we want the home-screen path. React is fine. React Native is not.

The revised spec

Same game. The changes are aimed at one thing: put AI where it does double duty, making the product better and filling the 40-point hole at the same time. Three additions, four subtractions.

Addition A. The garden grows from memories, not quiz scores

Replace quiz-to-earn as the primary loop. Instead of answering trivia for coins, the player tells the garden one small thing about their life, and a plant is composed from what they said. Pick a heritage object, a kopitiam cup, a Setron TV, a sewing machine, answer two or three gentle prompts, and the species, colours and ornaments of the resulting plant come out of the answers. Tap any plant later and it replays the story.

Why this is the strongest single change:

  • It is literally worldbuilding, the word the rubric uses.
  • The evidence is better than brain training. Reminiscence therapy has a Cochrane review covering 22 studies and 1,972 participants, with moderate-quality evidence of small benefits to quality of life, cognition and communication in dementia.11 Small and honest beats large and unprovable.
  • It solves Zoe's exam problem. Nobody feels tested by being asked about their first kitchen.
  • It is culturally specific in a way a vegetable patch is not, which gives Miora far more interesting art prompts.
  • We already built it. Memory Garden's plant composer works today in the repo.

Keep light puzzles as a secondary earner for players who want more to do. Make them tile and match-based, not quiz-based, because that is what the genre research says seniors actually play.

Addition B. One NPC gardener who remembers you

The rubric names intelligent NPCs. Give it exactly one: a gardener, an auntie or uncle character, who greets the player, comments on what they planted, notices when they have been away, and refers back to a story they told three sessions ago.

The critical design constraint, and this is where most teams will get it wrong: do not build a free-text chatbot. A randomised study of 130 players found LLM-driven NPCs significantly increased cognitive load, with no significant improvement in the gaming experience, and measurable harm to usability and trust. Open-ended modules were the worst offenders.12 For a senior audience that is a disaster.

So: the NPC speaks in generated lines, the player answers by tapping one of two or three large chips. Generated output, bounded input. It stays tap-only, it respects our UX floor, and it still demos as obviously alive.

Addition C. Keep the outdoor nudge, drop the pedometer

Dewa's Pokemon Go instinct was right, the implementation was wrong. Replace "walk 1,000 steps" with "bring back one photo". The camera works from a browser. The player photographs a plant outside, a vision model identifies it, the NPC reacts to what they found, and it becomes a real seed in their garden.

This is better on every axis. It works from a web link. It is a second visible AI beat. It demos in ten seconds on video. And it connects the game to Community in Bloom and the real gardens seniors already visit, which is a much better story than step counting.

Positioning against Healthy 365

Do not compete on steps and points, we lose. Position on what Healthy 365 has nothing of: memory, story, and a character who knows you. Healthy 365 tracks a senior. This remembers one. If a judge raises the overlap, the answer should already be on a slide.

Kept as-is

Meal achievements, wilting instead of death, 2D, mobile-first, gentle pacing, no timers, the profile and garden sharing. Make the Meal Card itself generated, so what unlocks reflects what that specific player grew, and localise the recipes. An ah ma is more moved by a soup she recognises than by an Omega Bowl.

Best next move

Not code. Two things first, and they take a day between them.

One. Lock the spec on one page, then build a single vertical slice with CodeBuddy. Not the whole game. One screen, end to end: pick an object, answer two prompts, watch a plant appear, have the gardener say something about it. The reason to do this before anything else is that nobody on the team has used CodeBuddy, and it appeared in none of the training materials. It is the largest unknown in the entire plan and 40 points depend on it. Find out what it actually does on a small thing, today, rather than on day seven of a nine-day build.

Two. Start the deck now, in parallel. The AI creation description is not a slide we write on 8 August. It is where 40 points get argued, and the material for it is being generated right now while we make these decisions. Every CodeBuddy prompt and Miora iteration worth showing should be screenshotted as it happens.

Chasing the credits stays on the list but it is blocked on someone else, so it runs in the background and does not gate anything.

The one-sentence version

Keep the garden, grow it from memories instead of quiz scores, give it one gardener who remembers you, and prove CodeBuddy works on a single screen before betting the week on it.
