Overview

We had three directions on paper and no way to feel them. So Dewa handed the repo to Claude Code overnight with one goal: make all three playable by morning so the team can decide with thumbs instead of slides.1

To be clear about what this is not: this repo is not our submission. The hackathon mandates CodeBuddy for the real build and Miora for art. These prototypes exist purely so we pick the right direction before spending two weeks on it. Deadline for the real thing: Aug 9, 11:59 PM.

Everything below runs offline on your laptop with one command. No accounts, no installs beyond node, no network calls.

Run it

You are both contributors on the repo now. Pull, then:

node server.js

Open http://localhost:4173. You will see a hub with three big buttons.

Judge on a phone, not a laptop. Every screen targets a senior holding a phone at arm's length, so it looks comically oversized in a desktop window and exactly right on a handset. Two options:

  • Fastest: browser DevTools, device toolbar, pick a 390 x 844 phone preset.
  • Real thing: with the server running, get your laptop's IP (ipconfig getifaddr en0) and open http://THAT-IP:4173 on your actual phone, same wifi. The server binds all interfaces, so this just works.

One warning before you judge anything: play each game twice. Two of the three have memory beats that only fire on a return visit, and those beats are the whole point of their design.

Memory Garden, what to feel for

Grow two plants with different answers. Do they look different from across the table, not just up close? Tap a plant to replay its story. Close the tab, come back, is everything still there? This is the intergenerational one: the free text field is meant for a grandchild's hands.

Mahjong Kakis, what to feel for

Give it your name and play a round. Tap two tiles that do not match on purpose and watch how the table treats you. Then close it, reopen, and listen to the greeting. The tiles are the hook, but the kakis are the product; judge the companions, not the matching.

Scam Dojo, what to feel for

Play three calls in a row. Do they feel like three different scams or one script wearing hats? Tap a line that is actually innocent and see what happens. On one round, deliberately choose "Do what they say". If that path ever feels like punishment, that is a finding. Reload and check your shield streak survived.

Scoring

After playing everything twice, score each game out of 5 on four questions:

  1. Did it feel alive?
  2. Did the AI beat impress you?
  3. Would you proudly demo this on Aug 9?
  4. Can you see us building this for two weeks?

Ties break toward the game whose AI story is easiest to explain in one sentence, because 40 of the 100 judging points sit on Use of AI Tools.2 Bring your scores; Dewa has the full bake-off argument doc for the discussion.

What is real and what is mocked

Say it plainly so nobody judges on a false impression: every generated line tonight (banter, captions, scam calls, recaps) is template-plus-randomized-slots from content banks, routed through one function in shared/ai.js. No live model was called. That is a deliberate architecture: it runs offline, rounds still feel fresh, and the file carries a labeled SWAP POINT where a real model replaces one function body with zero call sites changing. When we build the winner, that seam is where Tencent's model goes, with the banks kept as the offline fallback.

What is genuinely real: the plant generator in Memory Garden is seeded procedural composition, memory in all three games is real persistence, and the difficulty dial in Mahjong Kakis genuinely adapts to how you play without ever showing itself.

How it actually ran, in one paragraph: a conductor session planned and verified but wrote no game code. Three orchestrator agents (one per game) planned and reviewed; coder agents under them wrote everything. All three games reached a committed playable core before any polish was allowed anywhere, so a mid-run halt would have been harmless. After all three hit their definitions of done, a nine-agent adversarial sweep attacked them (double-tap attacks, markup injection into the name fields, measuring every screen against the senior UX floor) and found 20 serious issues, including double taps that skipped whole screens and one stored XSS path. All were fixed and regression-tested before sunrise.3

Nothing here rests on an agent's word. Every claim was verified by driving the real UI headless at a phone viewport with scripted taps, and the full test battery was rerun from scratch at handoff: all suites green, all four routes clean.4

Known rough edges

These are documented limitations, not undiscovered bugs. Do not mark a game down for something it already confesses to:

  • Memory Garden: object descriptions in the picker are hidden at phone widths (every layout tried broke small screens); one plant silhouette (woven palm) is the least plant-like.
  • Mahjong Kakis: reloading mid-round resets that round (your name and stats survive); banter repeats eventually in very long sessions.
  • Scam Dojo: the streak counts rounds, not real calendar days; the daily framing is aspirational in v0.
  • Everywhere: art is placeholder CSS and SVG on purpose. Miora art comes later. Judge the bones, not the paint.
